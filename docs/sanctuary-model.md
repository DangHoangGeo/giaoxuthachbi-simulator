# Sanctuary model · 7 October 2026 (revision 5)

The interactive viewer builds the sanctuary from `sanctuary.js`, called before geometry batching; carved ornament comes from the shape generators in `carving.js`. The approved generated image and the original church photograph are saved in `Thach_Bi_Viewer/references/` and registered in the References gallery. This revision follows that image and section sheet 5 (“Mặt đứng một vì gian cung thánh”).

**Owner clarification, 7 October 2026:** the model did not yet reflect the latest approved sanctuary and column/beam connection art, so the [new church view set](../Thach_Bi_Viewer/references/00-overview/2026-10-07/index.html) combines the model layout with the richer approved concepts. **Revision 5 brings that art into the model in steps.** Done so far: the columns (see *Columns throughout the church* below). The carved beam connections and the richer sanctuary relief of views 05–09 are not in the model yet; for those parts the views remain appearance studies. Generated carving approximates the art. It is not scanned sculpture, it defines no joint or residual timber section, and it has not been structurally checked. See the [source hierarchy and model differences](church-view-renderings.md). No equipment moved and no calculated result changed in this step.

## Limits

- Sculptures, lattice and floral carving are procedural approximations, not scanned sculpture or construction joinery.
- Column capitals, bases, blooms and leaves are generated shapes (about 190 000 triangles for all columns). They sit inside the footprint of the existing pedestals and shafts, so walking clearances and the sightline study are unchanged. The simulator still models each column as its shaft and 0.82 m pedestal: capitals and base mouldings are not in the light or sound analysis.
- Removing the axis-10 tie follows sheet 5, but member sizes and the roof thrust on that axis need an engineer.
- From the wing benches (choir, ministers) the lined chamber walls and columns hide the crucifix. With the corpus set back in the niche, none of the 80 wing seats has a direct view of it (8 had before the niche). The altar, the celebrant, the ambo and the opposite shrine stay in view. Every nave seat still sees the crucifix through the 6.6 m opening and past the niche reveals.
- The two stone planters at the front corners of the dais, which were there before, stand between 11 wing seats on each side and the centre of the altar.
- The ambo microphone's feedback margin fell from 1.5 to 1.2 dB with the ambo nearer the nave loudspeakers. It was already below the 3 dB the checks ask for.
- With less light in the sanctuary, less is reflected back into the nave (about 5 lux). Four seats at the ends of the last row now calculate at 197 lux, where 200 is the aim and they had 201. The lamp that serves the outer two is already at full output; one more small fitting on the entrance wall would restore them.
- The night levels above suit the default exposure of 110 lux. If *Eye adaptation* is set higher the whole picture darkens and the centre can take more light again.
- Lux values are direct light from the planning photometry. Real lacquer and gilding are glossy: check glare and reflections on a sample before fixing lamp outputs.

The wing speech-clarity minimum (0.439 against a 0.45 target) is unchanged in substance: no loudspeaker-to-seat path crosses the new walls. Nave clarity rose to 85 % of seats at STI 0.60 or better (minimum 0.53), because the removed front row held the weakest seats. 99 % of seats reach 200 lux (364 of 368; minimum 197; wing minimum 210). The simulator checks also cover the night balance of the centre (recess, corpus, gilding, altar, statues), the gilding on the 7 tie beams and 14 side beams, the timber tone setting, and the lamp-output-only migration. Browser inspection confirms the day and evening rendering from the nave, the top of the steps and under the roof, with both timber frames.
