# Electrical walk-round: safety and efficiency review · 9 October 2026

Status: **DERIVED / CONCEPT / ENGINEERING HOLD**. This is a review of the default simulator model, zone by zone, with conditional calculations. It is not a wiring design, a compliance statement or permission to buy, build or energise. The supply, earthing, fault level, products, cable types and installation methods are still unknown ([questions Q01–Q15 and Q42–Q44](../engineering/questions-for-parish-and-designers.json)); every current, cable size and fault value below is a comparison on stated assumptions, to be replaced by the responsible electrical engineer's checked design.

## 1. Result in brief

- **Fixed equipment** (lights, fans, exit signs; catalogue maximum, known subset): **9,262 W**. With the 25 % planning reserve that is 55.9 A at 230 V / PF 0.9 and 65.8 A at 220 V / PF 0.8. The AV rack is not included: its mains input is unknown.
- **Socket circuits** add their ratings on top: 32 A indoors (P1, P2) and 64 A at the towers (P3, P4). On one phase everything together is **152 A**; shared over three phases it is about **51 A per phase**. A single-phase supply cannot carry the event points with the rest of the church.
- **Protection screens**: comparison breakers are chosen at 1.25 × design current, so no fixed circuit loads its breaker above 80 %; the fullest is L6 at 74 %. With an ideal supply, a far-end earth fault would trip a type C breaker at once on every circuit even at the smallest usual cable; the least margin is on V1 (134 A against 100 A). The real supply and feeder will reduce these currents.
- **Voltage drop and cable size**: in case S1 every lighting and fan circuit passes the comparison with 1.5 or 2.5 mm², with the load spread along the route as modelled. In the hotter, more bunched case S2, F2, L1, L6, V1 need a larger size. The two indoor socket circuits fail in both cases at 2.5 mm²: 7.2 % and 8.1 % drop with 16 A at the mid-nave outlet.
- **Energy**: the full evening service is modelled at **5,587 W**, 8.38 kWh per service and 335 kWh per month at 40 services. Exterior lighting takes 44 % of the lighting power in that scene. Cable losses are about 0.6 %.

How to read it:

- **Design A** is connected load ÷ (U × PF). **Breaker** is the smallest standard rating at or above 1.25 × design current; for sockets it is the circuit rating. No trip curve or product is selected.
- **Drop %** is from the board that feeds the circuit to its worst point, at 1.25 × the catalogue load of fixed equipment with the current falling at each tap, or with the whole socket rating at the farthest outlet. Formula and constants as in the [cable review](cable-load-review.md): ΔU ≤ 2 I (R + X) L, R = 23.7/S Ω/km, X = 0.08 Ω/km. **Budget** is 3 % (lighting) or 5 % (other) from DB-1 less 1 % kept for the feeder.
- **Area** is the smallest table size, not below the smallest usual size, that passes both the ampacity comparison (breaker ≤ table current × temperature factor × grouping factor) and the drop budget. It is one size for the whole circuit. The older route-level review can show a different size on a single route because it shares the drop between trunk and branch.
- **Far-end fault** is 0.95 × U ÷ loop resistance of line and protective conductor at the smallest usual size (1.5 mm² lighting, 2.5 mm² fans and 16 A sockets, 6 mm² 32 A sockets), circuit only. **Loop allowance** is the impedance still available for the supply, the feeder and the board before a type C breaker (10 × rating) stops tripping instantaneously. A negative or small value means the circuit depends on residual-current protection or a larger conductor. The earthing system and real loop impedance are unknown (Q02, Q03).

A ceiling fan moves four to five times more air per watt than the wall and exhaust fans. From speed 2 to speed 3 it takes 76 % more power for 40 % more air, and it is 7 dBA louder. **Cable losses.** At full-service currents the final circuits lose about 33 W of 5,478 W (0.6 %) with the smallest usual cables. **Standing loads.** The exit signs (15 W) burn continuously: about 131 kWh a year, which the service-only figures above do not show. Drivers, amplifiers, the mixer and control gear also draw power when idle; none of it is known. Measure it at commissioning and provide one clearly marked switch that isolates everything except life-safety circuits.

## 7. Method and limits

- Source: the default model loaded headless (`scripts/lib/study_model.cjs`), through `scripts/export_electrical_loads.cjs` (which first checks that the model equals the exports and the six registers) and `scripts/export_electrical_safety.cjs`. A saved browser layout is a different configuration and is not read.
- Formulas, the ampacity table, the breaker ratings and the two comparison cases are those of the existing [cable review](cable-load-review.md), imported from `scripts/electrical_review.py`. They are international comparison references, not verified Vietnamese requirements; the adopted QCVN/TCVN editions and clauses are not established for this project (ENG-001).
- Fixed-equipment watts are catalogue planning values, not nameplates. Inrush, harmonics, power factor per product, starting currents of fan motors and LED-driver leakage currents (which matter for residual-current devices) are not modelled.
- Fault currents omit the supply, the earthing system, the feeder and the boards, so they are upper values. Disconnection times, selectivity, short-circuit withstand and surge protection are not checked.
- Zones, reach and weather exposure come from item positions and the model's floor levels and room envelope. They show where to look; they are not a survey and not an ingress-protection assessment.
- The separation screen compares route centre lines. Routes drawn on the same line may be meant for separate compartments of one containment; that detail does not exist yet.
- Energy figures are scene estimates. The three-phase allocation is one balanced example, not a board schedule.

Not checked in this review: earthing and bonding of metal chandeliers, fans and tower fittings; lightning protection; emergency lighting; fire stopping; conduit fill and supports; the AV rack supply; control hardware and its failure states; anything on site.
