The current design exposes the masonry around the silver M lunette and shutters. The timber and bronze surround and coloured glass perimeter have been removed. The shutters have solid red-brown fields under the diamond and pointed relief. [Current nave and porch views](masonry-reveal.jpg)

## Earlier design studies

In the initial study twelve nave windows share the doors’ red-brown timber materials. Both faces have a bevelled arch, bronze fillet and a lotus-and-vine carving in the fixed head. An open diamond lattice replaces the two upper solid panels of each leaf. The moulded lower apron and lattice move together. Lower pane reflections let the panel grain show through in daylight and under the evening lamps. Timber, glazing and carving specifications await physical samples and craft review. The subsequent nave colour study adds a bottle-green perimeter with amber corners and oxblood accents in the coloured-glass setting. [Day and evening from the same interior position](interior-colour.jpg)

The nave-facing casing now has a rounded timber bead and a shallow hollow that follow the arch down to both jambs. The bead shares the main door leaf material. [Same-camera detail comparison and day/evening nave views](nave-casing.jpg)

The latest option closes the timber field behind the diamond and pointed crown. The mouldings appear on both faces of every shutter. The original silver M remains in the fixed lunette. The review capture masks that artwork under H8. [Closed shutters from nave and porch](solid-shutters.jpg)

The current finish removes the coloured perimeter from the lower panes. The solid field shares the main door leaf material and clear body glazing now uses display opacity 0.006. [Nave comparison](clear-sash.jpg)
